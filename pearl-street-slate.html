From ledge to table, four miles.

Granville has shipped colored slate to the world since the 1850s — it's the only place in the country where sea green, purple and mottled slate come out of the same hills. Most of that rock becomes roofs and floors.

Ours becomes the middle of your table. Owner Paul Labas has spent his working life in Slate Valley rock, and every Pearl Street board starts as quarry ledge on Warren Switch Road, gets cleaved by hand with the same rivet-and-chisel technique quarrymen have used here for 170 years, then trimmed, eased and oiled in the shop.

No two boards split the same. That's the point.

How a board is made

  1. Block is cleaved from the ledge and brought down to the saws.
  2. Hand-split along the grain — the face you serve on is the natural cleft.
  3. Trimmed to size, edges eased so it handles like a board, not a shingle.
  4. Finished with slate oil; ready for cheese, charcuterie or the chalk line with your guests' names on it.

Slate is the easy houseguest.

Rinse it, dry it, oil it a couple times a year. No polish, no sealant, no fuss — this is the same stone that shrugs off a century of weather on a roof.

Chalk wipes off with a damp cloth. Scratches? A drop of slate oil and they disappear into the cleft.

Good to know

  1. Food-safe natural stone — serve cheese, bread, oysters, dessert directly on it.
  2. Hand-split faces vary — your board is literally one of a kind.
  3. Slate holds cold: 20 minutes in the fridge before serving keeps cheese happy.
  4. It will outlast the table you put it on.
