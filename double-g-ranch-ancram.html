Ways to board

Boarding built around the horse in front of us.

Some horses want a stall and a routine. Some just want grass and a run. Some are retired and need eyes on them every day. We board all three.

Full-Care Stall

Roomy windowed box stalls up to 16×16, daily individual turnout, mucking, hay and grain to your feed plan, and blanket changes. You ride — we handle the rest.

Pasture Board

Daily pasture turnout with shelter for the horse that lives happier outside. Same water, hay, and a real set of eyes on them every morning and night.

Retirement Board

Full-care retirement for the older horse — soft ground, quiet company, and someone who notices the small changes before they become vet calls.

Arena Board

Discounted board for horses that mainly work in the ring, with access to both the lit indoor and the outdoor arena year-round.

What full-care actually means here

The barn runs whether you make it out or not.

Boarding a horse an hour from home only works if you trust the people doing the daily part. Here's what that looks like.

01

Eyes on, every day

Someone is here seven days a week — feeding, turning out, and checking every horse morning and night. No skipped days.

02

Turnout that matters

Daily individual and pasture turnout on real acreage. Horses move, not just stand in a stall waiting for you.

03

Your feed, your way

Grain, supplements, and hay fed to the plan you and your vet set — not a one-size bucket.

04

All disciplines welcome

English or western, trail or ring, green or seasoned — every breed and rider fits in this barn.

The facility

Ride year-round, rain or January.

The 200×80 indoor is enclosed, lit, and windowed, with comfort footing that stays rideable when the weather won't cooperate. Outside, there's an all-weather outdoor ring and trails that run down along the Roe Jan Kill.

Windowed stalls so horses can see out. Wash and tack space. Trailer parking for hauling to shows. And the kind of quiet you only get on a working farm well off the main road.
