Before You Drop Off

Field care is the whole game. Here's how to protect your trophy.

Most ruined mounts are lost in the first few hours — not in the studio. Do these three things and call me; I'll walk you through the rest.

01

Don't cut the throat

For a shoulder mount, leave plenty of cape. Never slit the throat or brisket — that cut lands right where the seam shows. When in doubt, stop and call.

02

Keep it cool & dry

Get the animal out of the heat fast. Wipe off blood, keep the hair dry, and don't leave it in a warm truck bed. Hair slips when it's warm and wet.

03

Bag it & freeze it

Can't get to me right away? Double-bag the cape or fish, press out the air, and freeze it solid. Then reach out to set a drop-off.

Fish and birds are even more time-sensitive — text me a photo from the field and I'll tell you exactly how to handle it before anything spoils. That call is free, and it's the difference between a great mount and a missed one.

About the Studio
"I came to taxidermy through sculpture and painting — so I treat every mount as a piece, not a part."

Marissa built her own degree at SUNY New Paltz, weaving sculpture, painting and taxidermy together, then opened Styx River Taxidermy in Jay in 2019. Her work has been featured in Adirondack Life. Because it's a one-person studio, the hunter who drops off the cape talks to the same person who poses, sculpts and paints the finished mount.
