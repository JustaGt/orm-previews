Days on the water

The Upper Delaware is a wild-trout tailwater — no stocking, real hatches, and fish that have seen a fly before. Trips are built around the water level, the bugs and where you are as an angler.

Drift-boat float trips

Cover water on the West Branch, East Branch and Main Stem from the boat — the best way to reach wild browns and rainbows that hold where you can't wade.

Walk & wade trips

Wade the Upper Delaware, or the Catskill classics — the Beaverkill, Willowemoc and Neversink — on foot, working the runs a section at a time.

Spey & casting instruction

Single-hand, switch and two-handed spey casting taught on the water, whether you're refining a cast or starting from the basics.

New to fly fishing

First time holding a fly rod? Trips are paced for novices too — knots, reading water and your first wild trout, without the intimidation.

Wild-trout tailwater

Why anglers keep coming back to the West Branch

Cold releases from the reservoirs keep the West Branch fishable and full of bugs when other rivers go warm and slow. It produces blizzard hatches and selective wild trout — technical, rewarding water. Wayne is licensed and permitted in New York, Pennsylvania and by the Upper Delaware National Park Service, so a trip can follow the fish across the whole system.
