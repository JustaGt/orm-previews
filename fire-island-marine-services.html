Why one call instead of two

We carry our own machines to our own jobs

Most marine contractors working the barrier beach have to hire a barge to get their equipment across, which means their schedule is set by somebody else's boat. We operate both sides of that job.

For contractors and builders

Book the deck for your plant and material, and know the operator understands what happens at the other end — because we build there too. Tide windows, landing points and offload access get planned, not discovered.

For homeowners

If the bulkhead is going and the old one has to leave the island, that is normally three vendors and three invoices. Here it is one scope: demolition, transport, new structure, debris off.

How it actually works

Scheduling a barge job across the bay

Worth understanding before you plan a build calendar around it.

  1. Tell us the loadDimensions, weight, what it is, and where it has to land. That determines the vessel and the crew.
  2. We check the landingNot every community landing takes every machine. Access at the receiving end is usually the real constraint, not the deck.
  3. Tide and windowLoaded draft and tide decide the departure window. This is why "any morning next week" is rarely a real answer.
  4. Permits where neededDredging and marine construction are permitted work. We tell you at quote stage what applies to your scope.
  5. Run and offloadOut of Maple Avenue, offloaded at the landing, and — if it is part of the scope — the old material comes back on the return.
