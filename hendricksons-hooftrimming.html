Lameness is a quiet expense

A lame cow eats less, milks less, and leaves the herd sooner. Regular trimming catches sole and claw problems before they show up in the tank.

Trimming is herd management

Balanced claws carry weight the way they should. Functional trimming is about locomotion and longevity — not just overgrowth.

A crew, not a one-man wait list

Six trimmers means whole-herd days get done in days, not weeks — and your schedule doesn't slip a season when one truck is down.

What a Hendrickson's visit covers

Straightforward hoof work, done cow by cow and recorded so you know where your herd stands.

Maintenance trimming

Routine whole-herd or group trimming to keep claws at working length and shape through every stage of lactation.

Functional balancing

Weight-bearing corrected across both claws so cows stand square, walk sound, and hold condition on concrete.

Treatments, wraps & blocks

Topical treatment, wrapping, and blocking for sole ulcers, digital dermatitis, and injured claws — dealt with at the chute, same visit.

Lameness follow-up

Problem cows flagged and rechecked so a treated foot actually heals instead of turning into a cull decision.
