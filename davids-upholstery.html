Reupholster, or replace?

New furniture at the price point of old furniture is particleboard, staples, and foam that flattens in a season. The solid-hardwood frame you already own will outlast three of them.

Reupholstery costs a fraction of comparable new quality, keeps a piece you actually like, and lets you pick the exact fabric instead of settling for what's in stock. That's why customers drive to Barrington from Rochester, Elmira, and Syracuse — and why folks who winter in Florida haul furniture south after we finish it.

Bring us a photo, or the piece itself

Not sure if a piece is worth redoing? Send a photo or stop by the shop. We'll tell you honestly whether it's a good candidate, walk you through fabric, and give you a straight quote — no charge to ask.

A Barrington family trade

David Hoover's father started the upholstery business in the mid-1980s. David took it over, and today his son Earl Ray works alongside him — three generations at the same bench.

The shop grew up next to The Windmill Farm & Craft Market, which David helped build in the 1980s. On Saturdays, when The Windmill is busy, customers drop off and pick up while they shop. Word of mouth has carried the rest — people find us because someone showed them a chair we redid.
