Six metals, one bench

Most shops around here will do mild steel and stop. Aluminum, stainless, cast iron, copper and brass each need different technique — and that's usually exactly what the odd job needs.

SteelMild and structural — brackets, frames, plate work
Cast ironRepairs to old machine bodies, stoves, brackets
AluminumLight frames, trailer parts, guards
StainlessFood-contact, wet and outdoor work
CopperFittings, decorative, one-offs
BrassBushings, hardware, restoration parts

Cut. Drill. Machine. Weld.

Four operations that cover most of what walks in the door.

Cutting

Stock cut down to size, plate shaped to a template, old assemblies cut apart to get at the part that failed.

Drilling & boring

Hole patterns to a drawing, re-drilling worn mounts, boring out seized fasteners.

Machining

Turning and milling a part to spec — including when the original part no longer exists to buy.

Welding

Joining and repairing across all six materials, including the ones that don't forgive a bad pass.

The kind of work that actually comes in

Not production runs. Single parts, awkward repairs, and the thing the manufacturer stopped making in 1987.

Custom construction brackets

Timber-frame and barn connections cut to a builder's drawing — a real job that came through this shop.

Steel table legs & furniture frames

Bases for slab tables, benches and shelving, sized to the top you already have.

Farm & equipment repair

Cracked implement frames, broken mounts, worn linkages — welded and reinforced instead of replaced.

Discontinued parts

Bring the broken original. If it can be measured, it can usually be made again.

Trailer & hitch work

Frames, tongues, mounting points, guards.

Cast iron rescues

Old stove parts, machine housings and brackets that most shops turn away.
