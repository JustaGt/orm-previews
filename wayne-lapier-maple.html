From tap to jug

Real wood-fired syrup takes time and a lot of sap. Here's what happens between the sugar maples and the bottle you open.

Tap the woods

Late winter, the family taps the same Champlain Valley sugarbush worked for years.

Gather the sap

It takes roughly 40 gallons of clear sap to make a single gallon of syrup.

Fire the evaporator

We boil it down over a wood fire — the method that gives the syrup its character.

Grade & jug

Filtered, graded by color, and jugged fresh — ready for the table or the mailbox.

A family sugarhouse

Wayne LaPier & Family have been making maple syrup in West Chazy the hands-on way — tapping, gathering and boiling over wood. It's a season the whole family works, and every jug comes out of one small sugarhouse on Route 22, not a factory line. That's the difference you can taste, and the reason folks come back each spring.
