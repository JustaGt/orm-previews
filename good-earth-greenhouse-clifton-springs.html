Why this greenhouse is different

Curated, not stacked to the ceiling

A garden center orders pallets of the same ten plants. Good Earth works the other way around — chasing down the interesting ones, growing a modest number of each, and only selling what's earned its place. Here's what that means for you.

🔎

Varieties you have to hunt for

Owner-grower Terri Sauerhafer tracks down new and different flowers and vegetables from around fifty sources — the plants most nurseries never bother to carry.

🌱

Trialed before it's sold

New plants get grown out for a year first. If a variety doesn't perform, it doesn't earn shelf space. You get the ones that made the cut.

⏳

Small batches, so come early

We don't grow a lot of any one thing. The upside is real variety; the catch is the good stuff goes fast — early-season visitors get the best pick.

How to shop it

Come with a plan, leave with a surprise

Good Earth rewards the gardener who shows up curious. A quick way to get the most out of a visit:

Getting the good stuff

  • Come early in the season — small quantities sell through fast.
  • Ask Terri. If you're after something specific or unusual, she'll know whether it's coming.
  • Check what's new before you drive out — the benches change week to week.
  • Bring a box. When you find the variety you've been chasing, you'll want more than one.
