An attraction since 1890

Walk the streets of a city built 320 million years ago.

Rock City Park is the world's largest exposure of quartz conglomerate — house-sized boulders split into crevice "streets," an Ice Cave that stays cool in July, and a 45-minute trail your kids will call a maze and geologists call a pilgrimage.

What you'll squeeze through, climb past, and photograph

These aren't roadside rocks. They're the rim of an ancient inland sea, pushed up and cracked apart into passages you walk through single file.

🧊

The Ice Cave

A deep crevice that hoards winter well into summer — step down and feel the temperature drop twenty degrees on a July afternoon.

🪨

Crevice Streets

Boulders the size of houses, split into narrow walkways. Turn sideways, mind your shoulders, and come out the other side grinning.

🌄

The Overlooks

From the rim you can see three states' worth of Enchanted Mountains foothills — bring the good camera, especially in October.

💎

Museum & Gift Shop

Minerals, fossils, and the story of the trolley line that once carried thousands here. Every day pass includes both.

Since 1890, by trolley and by minivan

Before highways, a traction line — the local trolley — carried thousands of visitors up the hill each summer to picnic among the rocks. Ladies in long skirts squeezed through the same crevices your kids will run through today.

The trolleys are gone. The rocks haven't moved an inch. Rock City Park has stayed family-run and open to the public for 136 years, which makes it one of the oldest continuously operating attractions in New York State.
