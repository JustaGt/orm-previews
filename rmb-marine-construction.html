Before you call anyone

Four things that mean your wall is already working against you

Bulkheads rarely fail all at once. They tell you first. If you can see any of these from your back step, the wall is moving — and the sooner it is looked at, the more of it can usually be saved.

01

Sinkholes in the lawn

Soil is escaping through the sheathing and going into the canal. The hole in the yard is the last place it shows up, not the first.

02

The cap is out of line

Sight down the top of the wall. A bow or a dip means tiebacks or deadmen behind it have let go.

03

Gaps between boards

Daylight or water running through the face at low tide. Refacing may still be an option at this stage. It usually is not later.

04

The neighbour just replaced theirs

Canal walls fail in runs. Tying a new wall into an old one at the property line is a job worth planning, not discovering.

The part nobody warns you about

Permits, handled here

Waterfront work on the South Shore is permitted work. For most homeowners this is the part that stalls the project — not the construction. We have a permit expediter available, so the filing does not land on your kitchen table.

  1. Site visit and measurement. We look at the wall, the tie-in at both property lines and the access route for equipment.
  2. Scope and price. Reface or replace, in writing, with what each option assumes.
  3. Filing. Our expediter prepares and submits the application package.
  4. Build. Scheduled once the approval is in hand, so your dates are real dates.

Ask any contractor who quotes you one question: who files the permit? If the answer is “you do,” that is months of your summer.
