How we got here

One family, one farm, four decades ahead of the market

  • 1911

    The family starts farming this ground

    The same fields on West River Road that the herd grazes today.

  • 1981

    The chemicals stop

    No pesticides, no herbicides, no chemical fertilizer, no hormones, no antibiotics, no animal by-products — a decision made twenty years before there was a market that rewarded it.

  • 1984

    Certified organic — the first dairy farm in the country

    Certified by Vermont Organic Farmers (NOFA-VT). For the next twenty years the milk was sold into the conventional market anyway, because nowhere else would take it.

  • 2001

    Joined CROPP Cooperative

    The milk finally started shipping to Organic Valley in August of that year.

  • Today

    Kevin, Lisa, Joe and John

    All four work the farm. The store and kitchen run out of the restored Creamery building — a working dairy building since around 1900.

What that certification actually means here

No chemicals. Ever. On the land or the animals.

The herd

Dairy cows are never confined and move to fresh grass daily through the grazing season. Beef cattle are 100% grass-fed April to mid-November; over winter, organic hay and haylage, with corn silage at roughly 5% of the diet.

The certificate

Certified organic by Vermont Organic Farmers (NOFA-VT) since 1984. Milk ships to Organic Valley through CROPP Cooperative.

The rest of the shelf

Chicken from Jordan Farms. Eggs from Skytop Farm. Apples and pears from the Finger Lakes. Local honey, maple and coffee. If we didn't grow it, we'll tell you who did.
