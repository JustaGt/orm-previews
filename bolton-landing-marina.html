How it works

No trailer. No ramp line. No backing down a launch in front of an audience.

Dry stack is common on the coast and still new on Lake George. Here's exactly what a Saturday morning looks like for a rack member at BLM.

1

You call ahead

Let the dock know when you want the boat. That's the whole reservation.

2

Forklift lifts it off the rack

Your hull comes off a steel rack inside the storage building — dry, out of the sun and the weather.

3

Straight to the lane

Our handler drives it to a launching lane at the water's edge and sets it in.

4

Dockhands take over

Lines, fenders, a hand off the dock. You step on and go.

5

Come back and hand it over

Pull in, step off. It goes back on the rack. You drive home with an empty hitch.

5 min

That's the honest number, start to finish. The point of dry stack isn't the storage — it's that a spur-of-the-moment afternoon on the lake stops being a two-hour production.

Dry stack vs. the alternatives

Where a boat lives on Lake George

Slips are wonderful and expensive. Trailering is cheap and exhausting. Dry stack sits in the middle and most people have never had it explained to them.

Dry stack at BLM

Boat on a steel rack inside a building, launched on demand by forklift.

  • Out of the water — no bottom growth, no water intrusion
  • Out of the weather — upholstery and finish stay new for years
  • No trailer in your driveway, no towing on the Northway
  • Launched and retrieved for you, every time
  • Access to the clubhouse, showers, deck and grills

The usual routes

What most Lake George owners are living with instead.

  • Wet slip: the boat sits in the water all season, growing what boats grow
  • Mooring: a dinghy ride before and after every single outing
  • Trailer at home: the ramp line, the backing-in, the flushing, the tarp
  • Off-site winter storage: a second vendor, a second bill, a second phone call
The club side

BLM is family-oriented on purpose. The dock is where you keep the boat; the rest of it is where the day actually happens.

The gas dock is open to everyone

You don't have to be a member to fuel up or pump out. If you're on the water off Bolton Landing and you're getting low, pull in.

Gas dock and pump-out close one hour before the marina closes — please leave yourself the hour.
