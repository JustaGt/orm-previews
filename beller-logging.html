How a standing-timber sale actually works

Most landowners only sell timber a few times in their lives — so here's the process, in plain order, before anyone signs anything.

Walk

Gary walks the stand with you — what's mature, what's worth money now, and what should keep growing for the next sale.

Mark

Trees for harvest get marked so you can see exactly what a select cut takes — and everything it leaves standing.

Offer

You get a real offer based on the marked tally. Take your time with it; compare it if you like.

Harvest

The job is cut and skidded with the next 20 years of your woods in mind — landings tidied, trails graded when the work is done.

The select-cut promise: a properly bought woodlot should be worth selling again in 15–20 years. If a buyer talks only about this cut and never about the next one, keep looking.
One crew, start to finish

Land work that usually takes three contractors

Land clearing

Building sites, fields and lots cleared — timber value recovered from the clearing instead of burned in a pile.

Select cutting

Improvement harvests that take the mature and leave the growing — the difference between logging a woodlot and stripping one.

Grading & driveways

Once the land is clear we grade it and cut the driveway in — the same crew that cleared it, finishing the job.

Topsoil & sand

Screened topsoil and sand delivered for the site work that follows a clearing.
