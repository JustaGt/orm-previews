Why grown-here matters

A plant that was already living in Mount Sinai has one less thing to survive

The screening trees on a big-box lot were dug somewhere warmer, trucked overnight, and stood on asphalt in the sun for a week before you got there. Ours have been in this ground, in this weather.

We grow the stock

Fifty years of growing on this ground. We know which varieties get through a North Shore winter and which sulk.

We maintain it constantly

Plants get watered, cared for and looked at every day they're on the lot — not stacked and forgotten.

We'll say no

If the spot is too shady for what you picked out, we'll tell you before you buy it, not after it dies.

Design help

Start the conversation before you get in the car

The counter advice already works — bring photos, bring measurements, note the sun. The only problem is that it can't start until you've driven over. Now it can.

1

Photograph the spot

Stand where you'd stand to look at it. One wide shot is enough.

2

Measure it

Length of the bed or fence line, and how deep you can plant.

3

Note the light

Sun all day, morning only, or shade. This decides half the answer.

4

Send it over

We'll come back with a plant list, a count and a price before you visit.
