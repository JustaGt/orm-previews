Three letters most horse owners never think to ask about

Anyone can buy a rasp and call themselves a farrier — the trade is unregulated in New York. Certified Journeyman Farrier is different: it's the American Farrier's Association's highest standard certification, earned through written, forging, and live shoeing examinations, including building shoes from bar stock to fit a horse standing in front of the examiner.

That's the level of hand your horse stands on. Balanced trims, well-fit shoes, and an eye trained to catch small hoof problems before they become lameness.

How it works

The six-to-eight week rhythm

Hooves grow roughly like fingernails — steadily, whether it's convenient or not. Regular appointments keep feet balanced and catch trouble early, and barns on a standing cycle always know when the truck is coming.

  • Week 0

    First visit. Evaluation, trim or shoeing, and honest notes on each horse's feet.

  • Weeks 1–6

    Growth. You ride; the hoof does its thing.

  • Week 6–8

    Back on the books. Standing appointments — no chasing, no overgrown feet.
