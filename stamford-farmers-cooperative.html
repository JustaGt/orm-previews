Farmer spotlight

The co-op already writes the best local farm content in the county. Nobody can find it.

The existing Farmer Spotlight on Posthaven Farm in Hobart — eighth generation, registered Holsteins, the whole family named — is genuinely good writing that's buried at the bottom of a page.

Run one of these a month, put it on the front, and the co-op becomes the place eastern Delaware County reads about itself. That is the cheapest and most durable local search advantage available to a business like this.

What the spotlight series does

  • Gives every member farm a page that ranks for its own name
  • Gives the co-op fresh, dated content without inventing anything
  • Turns members into people who share the co-op's link
  • Documents a farming community that is genuinely shrinking
  • Costs a phone call and a camera, not an ad budget
Membership

Buy local by locals — and own a piece of the mill

Why members join

A cooperative is owned by the farms it serves. Membership is what keeps a grind-and-mix mill running in a village of 1,200 people, and it's the difference between a supplier and a shared asset. The current site never explains how to become one — a short page and a form fixes that.

What it means in practice

Member pricing, a voice in how the co-op is run, and a mill that stays open because the people using it own it. Specific membership terms, share structure and patronage details to be supplied by the co-op board before publication.

Call-ahead orders, finally in writing

The co-op already offers call-ahead ordering, payment by phone and delivery in some cases. Right now there's no way to start that online. This form does — it lands in the office with the species, the formula and the package size already written down.
